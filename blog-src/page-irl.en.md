## How the calculation works

- **New rent = current rent × new index ÷ reference index.**
- **Reference index:** the quarter named in your lease. If the lease names none, it is the last index published when the lease was signed.
- **New index:** the same quarter, one year later.
- The result is rounded to the cent.

## The rules this calculator applies

- A revision is possible once a year, and only if the lease contains an indexation clause.
- You have one year from the revision date to ask. If you do not ask within that year, you lose the increase for that year.
- The increase is not back-dated: it counts only from the day you tell your tenant. If the revision date was in March and you ask in June, only the rent from June on can be revised.
- A home rated F or G on its energy label (DPE) cannot have its rent revised (leases signed, renewed or tacitly renewed since 24 August 2022).

## What this calculator does not cover

- It does not read your lease. Check the clause, the reference quarter and the revision date it sets.
- It is for residential leases. Commercial leases use a different index.
- An increase at renewal of an under-valued rent, or after works, follows different rules.
- It gives information, not legal advice.
