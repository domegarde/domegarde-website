## How the deadline works

- You must return the deposit **within one month** of the tenant handing back the keys if the exit inventory matches the entry inventory, and **within two months** if it does not.
- The clock starts the day the tenant gives you the keys, in person or by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt.
- The amount you return may be lower than the deposit, because of justified deductions.

## What a late return costs

- The amount you owe is increased by **10% of the monthly rent, excluding charges, for each month started late**.
- To recover a late deposit, the tenant has to send a formal notice (*mise en demeure*).
- The penalty does not apply when the delay is caused by the tenant not telling you their new address.

## What you may keep

- Amounts the tenant owes: unpaid rent or charges, the cost of damage or of repairs not done. You must be able to justify them with documents such as the two inventories, quotes or invoices, or a letter claiming unpaid rent that went unanswered.
- In a building with shared charges you may keep a provision of up to 20% of the deposit until the building's annual accounts are approved. You then settle within a month.

## What this calculator does not cover

- It follows the general rule for a main-residence lease. Other lease types, or a missing entry or exit inventory, can change the rules.
- It counts calendar days and does not move a deadline for weekends or public holidays.
- It does not read your lease or decide what you may deduct. It gives information, not legal advice.
