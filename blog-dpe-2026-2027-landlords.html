Energy label (DPE) in 2026 and 2027: what changes for landlords, and what does not

16 October 2026 · By The Domegarde team · 4 min read · Last checked: 16 October 2026

The energy label of a rental home (the DPE, from A to G) decides whether you can let it, and since 2025 it has also frozen the rent of the worst-rated homes. Two things are easy to mix up: how the label is calculated, which is changing, and when a label bans letting, which is not. Here is where things stand on 16 October 2026.

In short

  • The letting bans have not changed. A home rated G cannot be let, or have its lease renewed, since 1 January 2025. F follows on 1 January 2028 and E on 1 January 2034. The ban applies to leases signed, renewed or tacitly renewed from those dates.
  • The calculation changes again on 1 January 2027. For electricity, the conversion from final to primary energy goes from 1.9 to 1.7 (it went from 2.3 to 1.9 on 1 January 2026). Some electrically heated homes can move up one class, in rare cases two. No home gets a worse label because of these changes.
  • A valid DPE stays valid. If the new calculation would improve your label, you can get an updated certificate free of charge from the ADEME observatory, without a new visit.
  • A bill would soften the bans, but it is not law. The Senate adopted it on 8 July 2026. The Assemblée nationale is still examining it.

What the DPE is for

  • You must give the tenant a DPE, as part of the technical file, no older than 10 years when the lease is signed or renewed.
  • A DPE made between 1 January 2018 and 30 June 2021 stopped being valid on 1 January 2025. It must be redone before you let.
  • A rental ad must show the energy and climate classes, the mention "logement à consommation énergétique excessive" for an F or G home, and the estimated annual energy cost. Service-Public says the landlord can be fined up to €3,000 (€15,000 for a company) in connection with these ad rules.

The letting calendar

  • G: no new lease, renewal or tacit renewal since 1 January 2025. A lease signed before then keeps running until its renewal.
  • F: from 1 January 2028.
  • E: from 1 January 2034.
  • Overseas (Guadeloupe, Guyane, Martinique, La Réunion, Mayotte): G from 1 January 2028 and F from 1 January 2031.

The rent freeze

For a home rated F or G, the rent cannot be revised or increased during the lease. This applies to leases signed, renewed or tacitly renewed since 24 August 2022 in mainland France, and since 1 July 2024 in the overseas departments. It is why our rent revision calculator stops when you pick F or G.

What changes on 1 January 2027

  • The electricity factor falls from 1.9 to 1.7, by an arrêté of 19 August 2026.
  • DPEs made from 1 January 2027 use the new value automatically.
  • DPEs already made stay valid for 10 years. If the new calculation improves the label, you can update the certificate free on the ADEME observatory.
  • Some homes could leave the F and G classes. That is not automatic, and it only matters for the letting bans and the rent freeze if the label really moves.

What a bill would change, and what we do not know

A government bill, the projet de loi visant la relance et la décentralisation du logement, is going through Parliament under the accelerated procedure (engaged on 25 June 2026). The Senate adopted it on 8 July 2026 and it reached the Assemblée nationale on 9 July. The Assemblée's economic affairs committee published its own text on 23 September 2026.

Article 6 of the Senate's text would change how the energy rule applies to a lease in progress. In short, it says:

  • a home under a running lease would be considered non-decent only from the lease's renewal or tacit renewal, and at the latest three years after the date the standard applies;
  • the home would still count as decent if works are impossible or disproportionate and the owner has done everything possible, if the co-owners voted the works down within the last 18 months, or if a works contract was signed before 1 January 2030 (three years to finish for a house or a flat, five years where the co-owners signed it);
  • these changes would apply to leases already running when the law comes into force.

We have read only the Senate's text. The Assemblée's committee may have changed it, and the final text may differ again. None of this is law today: the bans above still apply.
